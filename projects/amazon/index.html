01 / Problem

I started with a reporting process that had to be rebuilt every audit cycle.

Audit findings, site history and follow up status were spread across recurring files and separate systems. The team could find the information, but bringing it together into one useful view took repeated manual work.

The problem

The audit history existed, but it was spread across different places.

Understanding one site meant bringing together current findings, earlier audits and follow up information.

My goal

Build one view that could carry the history forward.

The team needed to see what happened before, what was still open, who owned it and what needed attention next.

02 / How I built it

I proved the workflow with a simple version before helping scale it.

I started in Excel because it let me build and test the idea quickly with real users. Once the workflow was clear, the project moved to Redshift for the larger data setup while QuickSight remained the reporting layer.

03 / Data flow

I connected the audit history to the work that happened after the audit.

The scaled flow brought audit data into Redshift and used QuickSight for the working view. Findings stayed connected to their follow up so the team could see both the audit result and what happened next.

04 / Data model

I structured the data around the full life of a finding.

I connected each site and audit to its findings, owners, due dates and follow up status. That made it possible to follow an issue from the audit through to closure and compare it with earlier audit cycles.

What I connected

SiteLocation and region
AuditDate, type and overall result
FindingIssue type, severity and audit link
Corrective actionOwner, due date, progress and closure
Action / ticketFollow up work linked to the finding

What the team could see

Open issuesSee what was still open or overdue.

Site viewKeep the same history between audit cycles.
05 / QuickSight view

I built the reporting view around the questions the team needed to answer.

I organised the view around the follow up work instead of copying the structure of the source files.

01

What happened before?

Previous audits and repeat findings.

02

What is open now?

Current findings, severity and outstanding actions.

03

What is being fixed?

Owners, due dates and progress.

04

What needs attention next?

Overdue or repeat issues that still needed follow up.

06 / Workflow

I kept each finding connected to the follow up work.

The platform did more than show audit results. Each finding stayed connected to its owner, due date, progress and related actions.

From audit to follow up

1
Audit added

The audit was connected to the correct site.

2
Finding added

The issue was mapped to a standard category and severity.

3
Follow up tracked

Owner, due date and progress stayed with the finding.

4
Action linked

Related actions and tickets stayed connected to the same issue.

07 / Outcome

I turned repeated audit consolidation into a view the team could keep using.

The final setup kept site history, findings and follow up connected across audit cycles. The team could work from the same history instead of rebuilding the picture from separate files each time.
